Lead

IAB Australia forces every crawler into one of four verdicts — and brands get their own column

Five categories (search indexers / training crawlers / live agents / ad infrastructure / unknown), four verdicts. Where publishers are pointed at block-or-license, brands are told to allow live agents on product and knowledge pages. robots.txt is explicitly labelled unreliable for live agents: Google ignores it for user-triggered fetches, OpenAI says it may not apply, Anthropic says all three of its bots honour it. Three operators, three answers, one directive.

The dated part: Cloudflare blocks Training and Agent categories by default on ad-displaying pages for new domains from September 15, 2026. TechCrunch reports it reaches existing free-tier customers too — cite the two separately, they are different sources.

The counterweight

Prompt injection as a brand-surface problem, not a security-team problem

Myriam Jessier argues the structural point: LLMs cannot reliably separate content from instructions, and that is a property of how they process text. Your help centre becomes a delivery mechanism; a competitor can weave instructions into a comparison article; any agent with untrusted input and a privileged tool can be hijacked.

Every Roadmap that recommends exposing an agent-addressable surface invites the question “what’s the downside?” This is the cited answer.

“if you’re giving conflicting metadata, you should fix it.” Companion: robots.txt block layered over a noindex means the noindex cannot be read — it caught a major AI lab’s own property.
