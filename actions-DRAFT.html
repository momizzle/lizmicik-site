The shift

The shortlist forms before anyone visits your site

Your next buyer's first move isn't your website anymore. They ask an assistant: "Who makes a 1500 kVA dry-type transformer I can get in twelve weeks?" The assistant answers from whatever it can read across the web, and hands back a named shortlist before your buyer opens a single page.

Here's the part that should get your attention. If the models don't know you, or they can't read you clearly, you don't make that shortlist, and you never find out. There's no missed-call slip. No abandoned-cart alert. The quote you lost was decided in a conversation you were never part of, about a product you make.

Being surfaced gets you considered. It doesn't get you chosen, and it doesn't let an agent do anything with you. Those are three different problems. Here's all three, in order.

Found is not chosen

Getting found is not the same as getting chosen

Say you clear that first bar and the assistant does surface you. Good. But being mentioned only gets you considered. To put your name forward, the assistant has to understand exactly what you do, and trust what it reads enough to recommend you.

Here's where it breaks. Your specs are written for eyes, not for data. A person reads the rating, the dimensions, the options, and understands the product in seconds. The agent sees prose, a table saved as an image, and a PDF link.

Then there's trust. If the model can't pin down who you are, or it finds one number on your site and a different one on a distributor's page, it hedges. And a hedging assistant recommends the competitor it understands better.

Seeing exactly what AI can read about you, where it's guessing and where it's flat wrong, across the major platforms, is what an Agent Readiness Audit is for. Most companies have never seen this view of themselves.

But even once an agent finds you, understands you, and trusts you, it still can't actually do anything with you. That's the gap almost nobody has closed.

Why agents get stuck

An agent can't just plug into the forms you already have

Most owners hear that agents are starting to buy things and think: I already built all of this. My products are on the site. I have a quote form. A person can use it, so an agent should just plug in and use it too. That feels obviously true. It's also where almost everyone gets stuck.

The website you built to talk to humans isn't something an agent can plug into. There's no plug yet, and here's why.

Your form is behind a wall built to stop bots

Your quote form often sits behind a CAPTCHA designed to keep bots out. An AI agent is a bot. It gets stopped at the door, the same one you put there on purpose.

The rules live in code agents don't run

A form's logic usually lives in JavaScript. The major AI crawlers (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot and the rest) don't run JavaScript at all, so they never see how your form actually works.

Nothing tells the agent what a valid request is

A salesperson fixes a vague inquiry with a phone call: "I'll need the phase and the enclosure rating." An agent can't. It needs the rules up front, in a form it can read. A human tolerates ambiguity; an agent needs structure.

None of this is a knock on your website. It reads fine to people. It just wasn't built to be read, or acted on, by machines, because until recently nothing was.

The fix

Three layers, and you keep everything you've built

Layer 1 — Your website

For people and for search. Nothing here changes. No rebuild, no redesign, no new software.

Layer 2 — Structured data

The same facts, marked up so machines read them correctly. The page a person reads as fifteen specifications, a machine finally reads as fifteen too, not three.

Layer 3 — The agent surface

The new one: a machine-readable place where an agent can discover what you quote, what it has to provide, and how to submit. That third layer is the plug your buyer's agent was looking for. Almost no one has built it.

Do it in order: get found, get understood and trusted, then get usable. Building that third layer, on top of the first two, is what Agent Readiness Actions delivers.
