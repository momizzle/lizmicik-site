Find: Can AI Access Your Website?

1 2 2 2

Before AI systems can say anything about your business, they need to be able to reach your website. This dimension checks whether your technical infrastructure welcomes or blocks AI crawlers, and whether you've set up the specific files and configurations that AI models look for.

AI Crawler Access

AI crawlers are the automated systems that models send to read your website. If crawlers can't get in, AI can't learn about you. These checks verify that your technical infrastructure welcomes AI traffic.

PASS robots.txt Allows AI Crawlers

A robots.txt file sits at the root of your website and tells web crawlers (including AI systems) which pages they can and cannot access. It is the first thing any AI model checks before reading your site.

What we found

Your robots.txt is permissive. The default rule (User-Agent: *) allows all crawlers full access. No AI crawlers are blocked. This is good. Your front door is open.

Technical detail

Raw robots.txt includes specific rules for Claude-SearchBot, Claude-User, and ClaudeBot with Allow: / directives. A 'Stop Claude' text artifact at the end appears to be from a WordPress plugin. All other crawlers fall under the permissive wildcard rule.

MEDIUM Selective AI Crawler Management (BV-011)

Modern AI platforms each send their own named crawler to read your website. Explicitly allowing these crawlers by name (GPTBot for OpenAI, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended) signals that your site welcomes AI indexing and can improve crawl priority.

What we found

Your robots.txt has explicit rules for Claude's bots but does not address GPTBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended, Bytespider, or other AI crawlers. While these crawlers are technically allowed under the wildcard rule, adding explicit Allow directives for each signals intent and can improve how thoroughly they index your content.

Technical detail

AI crawlers not specifically addressed: GPTBot, Google-Extended, Bytespider, CCBot, Anthropic-AI, PerplexityBot, Amazonbot. These all fall under User-Agent: * (permissive). The selective Claude-only approach may be from an early implementation before other AI crawlers became standard.

Recommendation: Add explicit Allow: / directives for GPTBot, Google-Extended, PerplexityBot, Bytespider, CCBot, and Amazonbot.

AI Model Handshake

Beyond basic crawl access, newer AI systems look for dedicated files that provide structured information about your organization. These are explicit channels for communicating with AI platforms on your terms.

LOW llms.txt Not Implemented (BV-012)

An llms.txt file sits at the root of your website and provides AI models with a structured summary of who you are, what you do, and how to describe you. Think of it as a handshake between your website and every AI platform.

What we found

Your site returns a 404 for both /llms.txt and /.well-known/llms.txt. This means AI models have no direct channel to learn your preferred positioning, key services, or how to accurately describe your firm.

Technical detail

Both root and .well-known paths return 404. Wordlift also flagged this as missing. Implementation requires creating a static text file and deploying to the web root or .well-known directory.

Recommendation: Create an llms.txt file with Jabian's core positioning, service areas, differentiators (local model, senior team access), target industries, and preferred description language.

LOW Cloudflare Markdown for Agents Not Configured (BV-015)

Cloudflare (your CDN provider) offers a feature called 'Markdown for Agents' that automatically reformats your web pages into clean, structured markdown when an AI crawler requests them. This makes your content significantly easier for AI models to parse and understand.

What we found

This feature is not currently enabled. Your website serves raw HTML to AI crawlers, which requires them to strip out navigation, styling, and layout code to find your actual content. Enabling Markdown for Agents is a low-effort, high-impact configuration change.

Technical detail

CDN analysis shows Cloudflare with cache_status: DYNAMIC (not cached at edge). Markdown for Agents was introduced February 2026. It is a dashboard toggle, not a code change.

Recommendation: Enable Markdown for Agents in Cloudflare dashboard settings. Test that markdown versions render correctly for key pages.

MEDIUM dateModified Freshness Signal May Be Inaccurate (SCH-007)

The dateModified field in your structured data tells AI models when your content was last updated. AI systems use this as a freshness signal, with more recently updated content receiving higher confidence in recommendations.

What we found

Wordlift's analysis flagged that the dateModified property in your Organization schema was set to 2026-02-06. Depending on when it was originally configured, this may have been a future date at the time, which signals an error or manipulation to AI agents. Understand: Does AI Get What You Do?

8 3 1 2

Once AI systems can access your site, the question becomes: do they understand what they are reading? This dimension checks whether your website communicates clearly in the language machines rely on: structured data, semantic markup, and content that answers the questions buyers ask.

Schema Implementation

Schema markup is the machine-readable vocabulary that tells AI exactly what your content represents. Service pages need Service schema. Case studies need CaseStudy schema. Leaders need Person schema. Without these explicit labels, AI must guess what each page is about.

HIGH Services Pages Lack Service Schema (SCH-002)

Schema.org Service markup is a structured data format that tells AI systems 'this page describes a professional service.' Without it, AI models must guess whether a page is about services, a blog post, or something else entirely.

What we found

Both the services overview (/service-areas) and the Strategy and Scale detail page (/service-areas/strategy-scale) use generic WebPage schema. Neither declares Service or ProfessionalService type. When a prospect asks AI 'What services does Jabian offer?', the AI cannot cite structured schema and must parse unstructured text instead.

Technical detail

WordPress theme defaults to WebPage type for all pages. Yoast SEO does not auto-generate Service schema. Requires custom post type schema template in child theme.

Pages affected:

  • https://jabian.com/service-areas — has_service: false
  • https://jabian.com/service-areas/strategy-scale — has_service: false

Recommendation: Implement schema.org/Service on all service pages with properties: name, description, provider (Organization), areaServed, serviceType.

HIGH Case Studies Have No Structured Data (SCH-003)

Case study schema (CreativeWork or CaseStudy) tells AI systems that a page contains evidence of your work: who the client was, what industry they are in, what you accomplished, and what results you delivered. This is the structured proof AI needs to recommend you.

What we found

The case studies page has zero structured data beyond generic WebPage. No client names, industries, outcome metrics, or engagement details appear in schema. AI systems reading this page cannot extract evidence of Jabian's expertise in a machine-readable format.

Technical detail

No client names, no industry tags, no outcome metrics in schema. case_studies-sitemap.xml last modified September 2025, which may indicate stale content. WordPress likely has a case_studies custom post type (based on sitemap).

Page affected:

  • https://jabian.com/resources/case-studies — has_case_study_schema: false

Recommendation: Add CreativeWork or CaseStudy schema to each case study with: name (outcome-focused title), description, client (Organization with industry), metrics (quantified outcomes), and serviceArea.

HIGH 18 Leaders Have Zero Person Schema (SCH-005)

Person schema tells AI systems that a page contains information about a specific individual: their name, title, expertise, and organizational affiliation. This is how AI builds authority profiles for the people behind a company, which feeds directly into E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authority, Trustworthiness) scoring.

What we found

The leadership page lists 18 named leaders (3 founders plus 15 executive team members) with no Person schema for any of them. There are 35 images (likely headshots) with only 1 alt tag. AI systems cannot determine who leads which practice, what their expertise is, or how to cite them as authority figures.

Technical detail

CollectionPage type is appropriate but no Person entities in the @graph. person_patterns: nameElements: 34, titleReferences: 15. Images: total 35, withAlt: 1. Named leaders include Brian Betkowski, Chris Reinking, Nigel Zelcer, Rob Amberg, and 14 others.

Page affected:

  • https://jabian.com/about/leadership — has_person_schema: false

Recommendation: Create Person schema for each leader with: name, jobTitle, description (bio with expertise areas), image, worksFor (Jabian Organization), and knowsAbout (expertise topics).

HIGH No Author Attribution in Content Schema (SCH-010)

Author schema connects content (blog posts, case studies, articles) to the specific person who created it. This tells AI that a thought leadership piece on AI strategy was written by an actual expert at your firm, not anonymous content. It is a key signal for credibility.

What we found

Blog articles, case studies, and service pages contain no author attribution in schema. Blog posts have no Article/BlogPosting schema that would include an author field. Case studies do not identify which consultants delivered the results. This breaks the chain between your content and your people.

Technical detail

case_studies: 'No client names, no specific metrics, no Person schema connecting expertise to individuals.' blog: 'No Article or BlogPosting schema for individual posts [which would include author field].' strategy_scale: 'no Person schema connecting expertise to individuals.' Pages affected: /service-areas/strategy-scale, /resources/case-studies, /resources/blog.

Recommendation: Add author properties to Article schema on blog posts (linked to Person schema). Add contributors to case studies linking to the consulting team members involved.

MEDIUM Organization Schema Missing Contact Properties (SCH-001)

Organization schema is the foundation of how AI systems understand your company. Beyond your name and type, it should include how to reach you: physical addresses, phone numbers, and email contacts for each office location.

What we found

The Organization schema on your homepage, about, and contact pages declares the Jabian entity but lacks address and contactPoint properties. AI agents cannot extract office addresses or contact information from structured data. Given Jabian's 6 office locations, this is a significant gap.

Technical detail

WordLift: 'Organization schema missing address and contactPoint properties'. Contact page declares Organization type but no ContactPoint schema. Jabian operates offices in Atlanta, Charlotte, Chicago, Dallas-Fort Worth, Denver, Houston. Pages affected: /, /about, /contact.

Recommendation: Add postal addresses for each office location and at least one telephone and email via ContactPoint schema.

MEDIUM Boilerplate Schema Across All Pages (SCH-006)

Ideally, each page on your website has schema tailored to its content type. A service page should have Service schema, a blog post should have Article schema, and a leadership page should have Person schema. Using the same generic template everywhere tells AI that every page is identical.

What we found

Every page includes the same Organization + WebPage + BreadcrumbList schema combination. This site-wide template from Yoast SEO was never customized per content type. AI agents see the same schema whether they land on a service page, a case study, or a blog post.

Technical detail

Schema matrix shows identical type list across 6 of 8 pages. Minor variations: service pages add an extra ImageObject, blog missing WebPage. WordPress + Yoast SEO + Divi Child theme. Yoast settings > Search Appearance > Post Types controls this. Pages affected: all 8 sampled.

Recommendation: Configure Yoast SEO to output page-type-specific schema per post type. Services should output Service schema, case studies should output CreativeWork, blog posts should output Article.

MEDIUM Blog Articles Lack Article Schema (SCH-004)

Article or BlogPosting schema tells AI systems that a page is a published piece of content with an author, publication date, and topic. Without it, your thought leadership is just another page with text on it.

What we found

The blog index has no WebPage type declared. Individual posts lack Article or BlogPosting schema. This is particularly damaging because Jabian has published relevant content like '5 Content Tips for GEO' and strategic articles targeting executive decision-makers, but AI systems cannot identify these as authored thought leadership.

Technical detail

blog page analysis: 'No WebPage type on blog index. No Article or BlogPosting schema for individual posts.' Yoast SEO has built-in Article/BlogPosting support. Check Settings > Search Appearance > Post Types > Post. Pages affected: /resources/blog.

Recommendation: Enable Article schema output in Yoast SEO settings for the Post post type. Ensure author, datePublished, and headline properties are populated.

LOW No LocalBusiness Schema for 6 Office Locations (SCH-009)

LocalBusiness schema tells AI systems about your physical office locations: where each office is, what services it offers, and how to reach it. For a consulting firm with multiple offices, this helps AI answer 'Is there a Jabian office near me?' with structured, accurate data.

What we found

Jabian operates 6 offices (Atlanta, Charlotte, Chicago, Dallas-Fort Worth, Denver, Houston). A locations-sitemap.xml exists (confirming location pages are in the CMS), but no LocalBusiness schema was found. AI systems cannot connect the firm to geographic locations in structured data.

Technical detail

locations-sitemap.xml lastmod 2026-02-23 confirms location pages exist. Assumed URLs: /locations/atlanta, /locations/charlotte, etc. Location pages were not in the 8-page sample but are confirmed via sitemap.

Recommendation: Add LocalBusiness schema to each location page with: name, address, telephone, geo coordinates, and parentOrganization link to the global Organization entity.

PASS Basic Organization Entity Declared

The most fundamental schema type is Organization, which declares that your website represents a business entity. This is the starting point for all other structured data.

What we found

Jabian correctly declares Organization schema on the homepage and major pages. Yoast SEO generates this automatically. The foundation exists for building richer, page-specific schema on top of it.

Semantic HTML Structure

Semantic HTML uses purpose-built tags (main, article, section, h1) to give AI a structural map of your content. Without these tags, your pages are a flat wall of divs with no landmarks for AI to navigate.

HIGH Missing H1 Tags on Most Pages (SEM-001)

The H1 tag is the primary heading on any web page. It tells AI systems and search engines 'this is what this page is about.' Every page should have exactly one H1 that clearly states its topic.

What we found

The homepage has no H1 tag (8 headings, all H2). Case studies page: no H1. Blog page: empty H1. Leadership page: no H1. Service detail pages start with H2 instead of H1. Without H1 tags, AI agents must guess which heading is the page topic.

Technical detail

Site-wide CMS/theme issue — most page templates do not output an H1.

Pages affected:

  • https://jabian.com/ — 8 H2 tags, 0 H1
  • https://jabian.com/service-areas/strategy-scale — starts with H2, no H1
  • https://jabian.com/resources/case-studies — no H1, all case study titles are H2
  • https://jabian.com/resources/blog — empty H1 tag
  • https://jabian.com/about/leadership — no H1

Note: https://jabian.com/service-areas has an H1 ("Service Areas") but uses H4 for all service category headings, skipping H2/H3.

Recommendation: Add a descriptive H1 to every page. Homepage: 'Jabian Consulting: Mid-Market Strategy, Operations, and Transformation.' Case studies: 'Client Results and Case Studies.' Leadership: 'Jabian Leadership Team.'

HIGH Only 1% Semantic HTML Usage (SEM-002)

Semantic HTML uses tags like article, section, nav, header, and footer to tell AI systems what each part of a page IS, not just how it looks. Without semantic tags, AI models see a flat wall of div and span elements with no structure.

What we found

Wordlift reports only 1% semantic element usage across the entire site. Pages rely almost entirely on generic div/span markup. The homepage has 7 sections but no main tag. Service pages have 0 section and 0 article tags. The Divi theme builder generates div-heavy markup that obscures content boundaries from AI parsers.

Technical detail

Wordlift: 'semantic_ratio: 1%'. Homepage semantics: section: 7, article: 1, main: 0. Service Areas: section: 0, article: 1, main: 0. Case Studies: section: 0, article: 1, main: 0. Blog: section: 0, article: 1, main: 0. Site-wide, all 8 templates affected.

Recommendation: Wrap primary content in main tags. Use article tags for repeating content units (case studies, blog posts, service offerings). Use section for grouped subsections. This is a theme-level change in the Divi child theme.

HIGH No Main Tag on Any Page (SEM-009)

The main HTML tag tells AI systems 'this is the primary content of the page.' It allows crawlers to skip navigation bars, sidebars, and footers and focus on what matters. Without it, AI may extract header or footer text as if it were body content.

What we found

None of the 8 pages sampled use a main tag. Every page shows main: 0. AI agents processing these pages cannot reliably distinguish primary content from navigation, footers, or sidebar elements. This is a site-wide structural issue.

Technical detail

Site-wide WordPress/Divi theme issue — no page template wraps content in a <main> element.

All 8 sampled pages affected (main: 0):

  • https://jabian.com/
  • https://jabian.com/about
  • https://jabian.com/about/leadership
  • https://jabian.com/contact
  • https://jabian.com/service-areas
  • https://jabian.com/service-areas/strategy-scale
  • https://jabian.com/resources/case-studies
  • https://jabian.com/resources/blog

To replicate: View page source on any page and search for <main. No results.

Recommendation: Add a single main tag wrapping all primary page content on every template. Implement in the Divi parent theme template or child theme override.

MEDIUM Blog Page Has Empty H1 Tag (SEM-010)

An empty H1 tag is worse than no H1 at all. It tells AI 'this page has a primary heading' but then provides no content, forcing the AI to ignore the semantic signal entirely.

What we found

The blog index page has an H1 tag that contains no text. Blog post titles are H2s beneath it. AI agents see a blank primary heading and must fall back to the page title meta tag to understand this is a blog.

Technical detail

Blog page headings: 'tag: H1, text: (empty)'. Assessment: 'Empty H1 tag. Blog post titles are H2s.' Pages affected: /resources/blog.

MEDIUM Decorative Use of H4 Tags (SEM-011)

Heading tags (H1 through H6) exist to create a content hierarchy, not for visual formatting. Using heading tags for decorative purposes (like numbering items) confuses AI systems about the actual structure of your content.

What we found

The About page uses H4 tags to display numbers (1 through 10) rather than for actual subheading content. This misuse of heading hierarchy signals false content structure to AI parsers.

Technical detail

About page: 'H4 tags used for numbering (1-10) rather than content structure. Decorative use of heading tags.' Pages affected: /about.

Recommendation: Replace decorative H4 tags with span or p elements styled visually. Reserve heading tags for actual content hierarchy.

PASS Leadership Page Uses Appropriate Heading Hierarchy

A well-structured heading hierarchy (H2 for section names, H3 for individual entries) helps AI systems understand how content is organized within a page.

What we found

The leadership page correctly uses H2 for section headers (Founders, Executive Team) and H3 for individual leader names. While Person schema is missing (see Schema findings above), the HTML heading structure itself is sound.

Form Accessibility

AI agents are increasingly capable of filling out forms on behalf of users. For this to work, every form field needs a clear label that tells the agent what data goes where.

HIGH Contact Form Has 75% Unlabeled Inputs (SEM-004)

Form labels tell AI agents and assistive technologies what each input field expects: name, email, phone number, company. Without labels, AI-powered form-filling tools cannot reliably map user data to the correct fields.

Trust: Will AI Recommend You?

9 5 0 1

AI agents don't just look at your website. They cross-reference what they find across platforms, compare you to competitors, and check whether your content is authoritative enough to cite. This dimension measures whether AI systems trust your firm enough to recommend it.

Schema Credibility

Schema markup doesn't just help AI understand your content (that's the Understand layer). It also makes verifiable claims about your business: who you are, where you're located, what services you offer, who your people are. When those claims are incomplete, inconsistent, or contradicted by what's actually on the page, AI agents lose confidence in your entire site. This section evaluates whether your structured data builds trust or undermines it.

HIGH Entity Identity Breaks Across the Trust Trifecta (SCH-012)

AI agents triangulate your identity by cross-checking structured data across your most authoritative pages: homepage, about page, and contact page. When these three pages tell different stories in their schema, or when the schema omits basic facts that appear on the page, agents flag the inconsistency and reduce confidence.

What we found

Jabian's Organization schema is present on the homepage, about, and contact pages (a good foundation), but it lacks the properties that build trust: no address, no contactPoint, no telephone. The contact page displays 6 office addresses and a phone number in the visible content, but none of this appears in the schema. AI agents see an Organization that claims to exist but won't say where it is. This directly undermines the trust signal that consistent, complete entity data provides.

Recommendation: Add PostalAddress and ContactPoint properties to Organization schema on the homepage and contact page. All 6 office addresses should appear in schema, matching the visible content exactly. This closes the gap between what AI can verify on the page and what the schema claims.

Technical detail

Organization schema present on /, /about, /contact but missing: address (PostalAddress), contactPoint (ContactPoint), telephone. Visible content on /contact includes 6 office addresses (Atlanta, Charlotte, Chicago, Dallas-Fort Worth, Denver, Houston) and contact form. Schema-to-content gap: structured data claims less than the page proves. Cross-references: SCH-001 (Understand layer), SCH-009 (LocalBusiness).

HIGH No Person-to-Content Authority Chain (SCH-013)

For AI to trust that your firm has real expertise, it needs to connect three things: a person (with credentials), their content (articles, case studies), and the organization they belong to. This chain is what builds authority profiles. Without it, your thought leadership is anonymous and your people are invisible.

What we found

Jabian has 18 named leaders, published blog content on relevant topics, and case studies referencing consulting engagements. But none of these are connected through schema. No Person schema on the leadership page. No author attribution on blog posts. No contributor links on case studies. AI agents cannot build the authority chain: Person → authored Content → Organization expertise. Each piece exists in isolation, and the sum is less than the parts.

Recommendation: This is a compound fix. Start with Person schema on the leadership page (SCH-005). Then add Article schema with author properties to blog posts (SCH-010). Then link case study contributors to Person entities. The chain builds incrementally, and each link strengthens the whole.

Technical detail

Leadership: has_person_schema: false, 18 named leaders, 0 Person entities. Blog: no Article/BlogPosting schema, no author field. Case studies: no contributor/author properties. The trust chain requires: Person schema (knowsAbout, worksFor) → Article schema (author → Person) → Organization schema (member/employee → Person). Zero links in this chain exist. Cross-references: SCH-005, SCH-010 (Understand layer), BV-010, BV-008 (below).

MEDIUM Schema Claims Less Than the Page Proves (SCH-014)

The opposite of schema overclaiming is schema underclaiming: your pages contain valuable, trust-building information that isn't reflected in structured data. AI agents can only use what's in the schema for structured reasoning. Everything else requires inference, which is less reliable and less likely to result in citations or recommendations.

What we found

Several pages contain rich content that the schema doesn't capture. The services overview describes 6 service areas, but schema declares only generic WebPage. Case studies describe real consulting engagements, but schema captures none of the client industries, outcomes, or service types. The blog has topically relevant articles with clear expertise signals, but no Article schema exists to surface them. In every case, the page proves more than the schema claims.

Recommendation: This finding reinforces the schema implementation recommendations in the Understand section. The priority order: Service schema on service pages (immediate buyer-intent queries), CaseStudy/CreativeWork on case studies (evidence of expertise), Article on blog posts (thought leadership authority). Each addition closes a gap between what the page proves and what AI can verify through structured data.

Technical detail

Services: 6 service areas described in content, 0 represented in schema. Case studies: 4 studies with problem/solution narratives, 0 with CreativeWork/CaseStudy schema. Blog: relevant titles ('5 Hard Questions Every Executive Should Ask Before Starting an AI Initiative'), no Article schema. This is a systematic pattern: the CMS (WordPress + Yoast + Divi) generates only default Organization + WebPage + BreadcrumbList regardless of content type. Cross-references: SCH-002, SCH-003, SCH-004, SCH-006 (Understand layer).

Create an llms.txt file
