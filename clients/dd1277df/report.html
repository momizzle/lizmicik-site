Executive Summary

The Shift

Your buyers increasingly start their research in AI rather than Google. When a facility manager whose transformer just failed asks ChatGPT, Claude, or Perplexity who stocks medium-voltage replacements for emergency delivery, or when a procurement engineer asks for a comparison of dry-type and pad-mount transformer suppliers, the AI builds its answer from your digital footprint. It uses your structured data, its understanding of your entity relationships, and the consistency of what you say across all earned web properties to do so. Unfortunately, AI agents do not often say “I don’t know,” or “I didn’t find that information.” Instead, they infer or guess with confidence.

ELSCO has already done a lot of the hard work needed. You have made sure agents are not blocked from your site, and you have made a significant investment in content. What you are missing is the structural backbone — the schema, the semantic HTML, the entity-graph cohesion — that translates that content investment into citation share on AI engines that don’t search the live web.

That structural gap is measurable. We use two outside audit tools (Gumshoe and Wordlift) as validation checks on our internal diagnostics. Both of these tools rank ELSCO solidly in the middle (35% and 47% respectively) in terms of AI visibility, noting the same issues we’ll discuss in detail in this audit.

Before we get into those details though, I want to take a moment to congratulate you. Your overall appearance and those scores are among some of the highest I have seen. 35% visibility on a scale of 100 may not seem especially great, but that means you are ranking 6th out of 120 brands mentioned across the medium-voltage transformer category. Within Gumshoe’s leaderboard, ELSCO beats every competitor named (Hammond, MGM, Maddox) and only legitimately trails Eaton (78%) and Schneider Electric (64%).

It’s what ELSCO needs most.

We incorporate the results from two outside AI audit tools (Gumshoe and Wordlift) in our audit to verify and provide further depth to the information we uncover. Gumshoe looks at your site from a content perspective. Wordlift evaluates your site strictly from a technical structure and schema perspective.

In ELSCO’s case these two independent measurement approaches converged on the same diagnosis of our own diagnostic points. The 75-point AI visibility gap between Perplexity (75%) and Claude (0%) (reported by Gumshoe) is not a competitive problem; it is a structural one.

ELSCO has provided a great deal of the information a human needs to make a decision to do business with you. However, machines cannot make the logical leaps that are relatively easy for humans to make, such as understanding your name change. We need to take extra care that the truly important pieces of information they need to understand us are presented in ways that are:

  • Clear — Clarity and specificity often make for very dull reading by humans, which is why we so often recommend adding a structured data layer to your important pages that speaks this precise language (schema).
  • Complete — Similarly, the more details you provide an AI agent, the easier it is for them to understand. A human knows that “M.D.” after a name means “doctor,” but AI agents need knowsAbout schema to tell them this doctor is a pediatrician. BTW, people appreciate this too.
  • Consistent — The more often they see a fact repeated, and the more places they see that fact repeated, lead to the verification and validation they need to declare something as a fact. Unfortunately, AI agents don’t know how to say, “I don’t know.” If they don’t see consistency, they will infer, and present half-truths or complete untruths as fact as well.

ELSCO has an opportunity to pull even further ahead of its street competitors by making sure that AI can represent you accurately in a broader array of queries.

Find: Can AI Access Everything on Your Website?

0 2 1 1 3

Before AI systems can say anything about your business, they need to be able to reach and read the content on the pages in your website. This dimension checks whether your technical infrastructure at your network, site and page levels welcomes or blocks AI crawlers. Can they pass through your Content Delivery Network (CDN), navigate according to your sitemap and robots.txt files, and reach or read your pages? AI agents visiting ELSCO don’t encounter any issues until they reach that third, page, level.

CDN Access and Capabilities

Before an AI agent ever loads a page from your site, it has to interact with the network edge — the CDN. Two things happen at this layer. The CDN’s bot-management policy decides whether the agent gets through, gets challenged, or gets blocked entirely (allow or disallow rules, applied per crawler). And the edge can also advertise capabilities to AI agents — alternative content formats, API endpoints, MCP servers, and the discovery manifests that announce them — that let the agent interact with your site as more than just a stack of HTML pages to parse. This topic group looks at how your network edge is set up to receive AI crawlers and what it offers them.

LOW Cloudflare CDN Detected, but Not Configured

A Content Delivery Network (CDN) serves your website from servers around the world, ensuring fast load times for both human visitors and AI crawlers. The CDN layer is also where modern bot-management and AI-friendly features live.

What we found

ELSCO runs on Cloudflare, one of the most AI-friendly CDN providers. Cloudflare supports AI-specific features including bot management, Markdown for Agents, AI crawler analytics, and emerging WebBotAuth signals — all of which are available on ELSCO’s existing plan.

Technical detail

cf-ray header on homepage HEAD request confirms Cloudflare delivery. server: cloudflare. cf-cache-status: DYNAMIC (homepage not edge-cached, expected for WordPress). The Cloudflare dashboard exposes a Verified Bots category in Bot Management that, if configured correctly, allow-lists named AI crawlers automatically. If the dashboard is on default settings rather than Verified-Bots-on, AI crawlers may be intermittently challenged.

Recommendation: Inside Cloudflare’s Bot Management dashboard you can toggle on a confirmation that allows AI bots to access your site. You can also confirm that CCBot (as well as the other bots you should recognize by name) is allowed. The Common Crawl bot feeds the web index on which AI foundation models are trained.

INFO Agent-Discovery Manifests Not Present (forward-looking)

A small set of standardized files at predictable paths (the /.well-known/ directory) is emerging as the way websites tell AI agents “here’s what is machine-callable, here’s how to address it.’ The IETF chartered the WebBotAuth working group in early 2026, and major CDN vendors (Cloudflare, AWS, Vercel, Akamai, Shopify) are implementing the related authentication signals on the same timeline. The manifests are not yet a citation lever today, but a 12-18 month forward-looking gap.

What we found

None of the standard manifests are present on elscotransformers.com: /.well-known/mcp.json, /.well-known/agent-skills/index.json, <link rel="mcp"> in the page head, or related agent-discovery surfaces. This is true of most B2B industrial sites today — ELSCO is not behind, but is also not yet positioned for the agent-callable surface that’s likely to matter in 2027 buyer journeys.

Technical detail

Endpoints checked and returned 404: /.well-known/mcp.json, /.well-known/webmcp/tools.json, /.well-known/mcp/server-card.json, /.well-known/agent-skills/index.json. Page head inspection returned zero <link rel="mcp"> or <link rel="agent-skills"> elements.

Practical implication: today, AI engines do not check or weight these manifests. By H2 2026 they begin to matter for agent-callable surfaces. By 2027, sites without manifests will be discoverable only through page-level content, not through the agent-skills layer.

Recommendation: Watch the IETF WebBotAuth working-group milestones (next major check: August 2026 BCP). When ELSCO is ready to expose agent-callable surfaces (a quote-request API, an inventory-check endpoint), add the discovery manifests to advertise them.

Server-Level Directives and Locators

Past the CDN, the next gate is your server itself. Well-formed sitemap.xml files tell agents about all the content you have. robots.txt files tell them what you want them to look at — or not look at. llms.txt files have been suggested (though not yet widely recommended) to help AI agents understand which content you’ve specifically aimed at them. This topic group looks at the directives and locators that tell AI agents what’s on your site and what they’re allowed to do with it.

PASS robots.txt Welcomes All AI Crawlers

A robots.txt file sits at the root of your website and tells crawlers (including AI systems) which pages they can and cannot access. It is the first thing any AI model checks before reading your site.

What we found

ELSCO’s robots.txt is permissive. The wildcard rule (User-agent: * with Allow: /) gives every named AI crawler full access to the public site. No Disallow directives target AI user-agents. The front door is open.

A note on explicit named-bot directives: it’s possible to add per-crawler rules (named Allow: lines for GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, and the others) to spell out AI-readability bot-by-bot rather than relying on the wildcard. Naming them explicitly mostly matters when a site wants to exclude specific crawlers — the explicit allow rules are how you make sure the “good bots” don’t get caught in disallow logic meant for someone else. Since ELSCO welcomes all AI traffic, the wildcard is the cleanest expression of that intent.

Technical detail

Raw robots.txt at elscotransformers.com/robots.txt contains: User-agent: * + Allow: /, three Disallow rules for WordPress administrative paths (/wp-admin/, internal search query strings, the newsletter path), and three Sitemap references (sitemap_index.xml, page-sitemap.xml, post-sitemap.xml). No named directives target individual AI crawlers (GPTBot, Google-Extended, Bytespider, CCBot, Anthropic-AI, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Amazonbot). All AI crawlers are covered by the wildcard. The Disallow rules for WordPress paths are appropriate exclusions and do not affect AI crawl access to customer-facing content.

PASS llms.txt Present and Well-Formed

The llms.txt file was proposed in 2024 as a plain-text directory of a site’s most important pages, formatted for AI consumption. Major AI crawlers (OpenAI’s GPTBot, Anthropic’s ClaudeBot, Google-Extended, PerplexityBot) have not adopted it as a citation signal, and several controlled studies in Q1 2026 confirmed that llms.txt presence does not measurably move AI-citation share.

What we found

ELSCO’s hand-built llms.txt is one of the better-formed examples in the wild. It follows the spec format with H1 page identifier, blockquote description, and section headings (Transformers, Transformer Services, Transformer Resources) covering ~37 entries. The descriptive text is accurate and the linked entries cover the priority product, service, and educational pages. This is real care taken at a layer most B2B sites have skipped.

That said: practitioner consensus through Q1 2026 (and three independent data points in early 2026) suggests llms.txt does not drive AI citations. ELSCO’s file is well-formed effort that does not need ongoing maintenance investment as a visibility lever. The right read: acknowledge the effort, do not credit it as a structural advantage, and put forward investment into the structural-data and earned-presence layers where AI-citation lift actually surfaces.

Technical detail

The file is well-formed and follows the spec. Top-of-file description: “ELSCO manufactures high-quality medium-voltage transformers in 500-2500 KVA ratings and 2400-34,500 primary voltages.” Three sections cover product pages (6 entries), service pages (6 entries), and resource/blog content (~25 entries).

PASS Sitemaps Declared and Well-Formed

An XML sitemap tells search engines and AI crawlers which pages on your site exist and when they were last updated. It is essential for comprehensive indexing.

What we found

ELSCO declares three Yoast-generated sitemaps from robots.txt: sitemap_index.xml (the parent index), page-sitemap.xml (199 page URLs), and post-sitemap.xml (~75 blog post URLs). Total inventory: ~274 URLs. The structure is well-formed, conventionally organized, and immediately discoverable by any AI crawler that reads the robots.txt.

Technical detail

Sitemaps located at elscotransformers.com/sitemap_index.xml, elscotransformers.com/page-sitemap.xml, and elscotransformers.com/post-sitemap.xml. All three are referenced via Sitemap: directives in robots.txt. Yoast SEO plugin generates and maintains them automatically on content change.

Page-Level Architecture and Blockers

Once an AI agent has permission to crawl, two things at the page level can still get in the way: the architecture that connects pages to each other (which an agent uses to navigate from one page to the next), and what’s actually on each page that might stop an agent from reading or interacting. Both are typically owned by your SEO and front-end teams — these are page-level fixes that don’t usually require backend access. This topic group looks at the page-level structures and barriers AI agents encounter once they’re on your site.

A full sweep of the 199-URL page sitemap surfaced four confirmed orphan pages parked at the site root rather than nested under the correct parent directory. None of these are duplicate-content issues — each orphan has its own page copy — but each represents a canonicalization gap (both URLs are self-canonical) and an IA inconsistency that weakens AI entity resolution.

MEDIUM CAPTCHA on Contact Form Blocks Agent Submissions

A CAPTCHA is a small puzzle a website asks visitors to solve before submitting a form — designed to block automated submissions. CAPTCHAs are, by design, built to stop machines. Any AI agent attempting to submit a quote request, follow up on an inquiry, or interact with a form on the site is blocked at the CAPTCHA step.

What we found

This finding is closer to a forward-looking caution than an immediate revenue impact. Buyer behavior in industrial B2B is still primarily human-initiated form submission, but the agent-mediated path is growing fast enough to surface in 2026 audits.

Technical detail

A Google reCAPTCHA is active on the homepage, attached to the Contact Form 7 plugin. The CAPTCHA was almost certainly added for legitimate spam control on lead-capture forms.

Recommendation: As B2B buyers begin asking ChatGPT or Claude to “contact ELSCO and request a quote for a 1500 KVA transformer,” the CAPTCHA blocks that request from completing. Consider changing to a more up-to-date contact form.

Understand: Does AI Get What You Do?

6 4 2 2

Once AI systems can reach your site, the question becomes whether they understand what they’re reading. This dimension checks the three things that build that understanding: structured data that tells machines what each page is, semantic HTML that gives them a structural map of the content, and entity coherence that confirms ELSCO is a single, well-defined business rather than a collection of overlapping claims. The pattern across this layer is consistent: ELSCO has invested heavily in content (274 URLs, programmatic local-SEO architecture, 75 educational blog posts), but the structural backbone underneath the content — schema markup, HTML5 semantics, and a coherent entity narrative — is at WordPress/Yoast defaults.

Schema Implementation

Schema markup is the machine-readable vocabulary that tells AI exactly what each page represents. Service pages need Service schema; product pages need Product/Offer; location pages need LocalBusiness; blog posts need Article or BlogPosting. Without these explicit labels, AI must guess what each page is — and on a WordPress site emitting only Yoast defaults, the guess defaults to “generic webpage.”

HIGH Schema Floor — Yoast WordPress Defaults Across All Sampled Templates

Across 12 sampled page templates spanning the entire site, the JSON-LD blocks emit only the three schemas Yoast SEO ships out of the box: WebPage, BreadcrumbList, and WebSite. No deliberate schema work has been added at the template level. Every page renders to AI engines as “a generic web page within a website” rather than as a service, product, location, article, or organization-bound entity.

What we found

The schema floor finding is the central UNDERSTAND-layer diagnosis for ELSCO. It is the single root cause that produces most of the other Schema Implementation findings below. Yoast SEO is doing its job — the defaults are correct, well-formed, and useful. But the WordPress + Yoast baseline is a starting point, not a destination, and ELSCO has not built upward from it at the template level. The two exceptions worth naming: the /about-us/ page emits an Organization schema (foundation finding) and two pages emit FAQPage schema (the Padmount Transformers product page and the Transformer FAQ page).

Technical detail

12 templates inspected: homepage, Medium Voltage Dry Type product page, Padmount product page, Emergency Replacements service page (and its duplicate at root), Transformer FAQ page, Hammond competitor-replacement page, About Us, Engineering Management Staff, Cincinnati location page, Manufacturing industry page, KVA-spec product detail page, sample blog post.

Every page returned WebPage + BreadcrumbList + WebSite. About-Us additionally returned a minimal Organization. Padmount and Transformer FAQ additionally returned FAQPage. The Engineering Staff page returned two Person schemas. The KVA-spec page returned WebPage+ItemPage with no Product or Offer children. A sampled blog post returned WebPage+ImageObject+Person with no Article or BlogPosting wrapper.

Recommendation: Build template-level schema for ELSCO’s six high-leverage page types (services, products, locations, blog posts, industry pages, and the homepage Organization graph). One schema build per template closes hundreds of URLs at once.

HIGH Template-Level Schema Absences Across the Highest-Leverage Page Types

The schema floor has the same expression on every page type, but the operational impact is different per page type. Below is the same root cause, surfaced as the four template-level absences AI engines actually feel: services without Service schema, products without Product/Offer, locations without LocalBusiness, and blog posts without Article/BlogPosting.

What we found
  • Services: The /services/emergency-replacements/ page (your strategic wedge) and the 12+ competitor-brand replacement pages (Hammond, MGM, Square D, Siemens, Eaton, ABB, Westinghouse, Hevi-Duty, Federal Pacific, Olsun, Vantran, Rex, GE VaporTran) all emit only Yoast defaults. No Service schema with provider, areaServed, serviceType, or hasOfferCatalog. AI engines see “a page about emergency replacement” but cannot bind it to ELSCO as the service-providing entity.
  • Products: ~40 KVA-spec product pages (e.g., /transformers/dry-type-transformers/1500-kva-13800-delta-to-480y-277/) are correctly marked as ItemPage but carry no Product or Offer children. SKU, availability, and pricing data that exists on each page (or could exist) is invisible to AI engines as structured product data.
  • Locations: ~80 state-and-city programmatic location pages (including the Cincinnati home-city page) emit only Yoast defaults. No LocalBusiness schema with address, geo, areaServed, or openingHours. Local AI-citation surface is effectively zero, and the programmatic location pages without LocalBusiness also read as a thin-content pattern to AI evaluators (see the Programmatic Location Pages finding in Entity Clarity below).
  • Blog posts: 75 educational blog posts emit WebPage+ImageObject+Person (Todd Benadum as author Person), but no Article or BlogPosting wrapper to bind the post to its author. Todd’s authorship floats disconnected from his published expertise. The 75-post technical library is invisible as authored work in the entity graph.
Technical detail

Each gap is fixable at the WordPress template level. Service schema, Product/Offer schema, LocalBusiness schema, and Article/BlogPosting schema can each be added once at the template level (typically through Yoast Premium, Schema Pro, RankMath, or a custom JSON-LD plugin) and apply to every URL using that template. Four template builds close the gap across hundreds of URLs.

Recommendation: Prioritize the schema builds in this order: Service (highest immediate impact — protects the strategic wedge), LocalBusiness (largest URL count + highest spam-pattern risk), Product/Offer (commerce-intent buyer queries), Article/BlogPosting (E-E-A-T binding).

MEDIUM Organization Schema Isolated — Graph Cohesion Gap

The single Organization schema on the site lives on /about-us/. The homepage WebSite schema does not reference the Organization @id. Pages on the rest of the site aren’t connected back to the Organization entity declaration. The organization exists in one room of the house with the door closed, where the front door (the homepage) and every other room (services, products, locations, blog posts) cannot reference it.

What we found

The Organization schema on About-Us declares name, alternateName, url, logo, contactPoint, and a sameAs array. Foundation fields are present but minimal: missing address, foundingDate, description, knowsAbout, areaServed, founder, numberOfEmployees, and hasOfferCatalog. The schema’s page-level description claims “industry leaders... since 1960,” but ELSCO has actually been operating since 1912 — a 113-year history that should be declared via foundingDate of 1912, with the description rewritten to match. Each page elsewhere on the site emits its own WebPage schema but does not reference the About-Us Organization, so an AI engine reading the homepage doesn’t learn that the Organization exists, and reading a service page doesn’t learn that ELSCO is its provider.

Technical detail

Standard fix pattern: assign the Organization schema a stable @id (e.g., https://elscotransformers.com/#organization), move the schema to the homepage so it’s the first thing crawlers encounter, and reference that @id from every other schema entity on the site (each Service’s provider, each Product’s brand, each LocalBusiness’s parentOrganization, each Article’s publisher). This converts the entity declaration from one isolated page to a graph reference that every other page resolves to.

Recommendation: Move and expand the Organization schema, assign a canonical @id, and reference it from every other entity in the graph.

PASS FAQPage Schema Present on 2 Pages — Pattern Works, Not Yet Extended

The Padmount Transformers product page and the Transformer FAQ page both emit FAQPage schema with question/answer pairs. The implementation is correct, the pattern is working, and AI engines reading those two pages can extract the FAQ content as structured Q&A.

What we found

FAQPage schema is one of the highest-ROI schema types for AI-citation purposes — it directly maps to the question-and-answer format AI engines use to surface direct-answer content. ELSCO has the pattern working on two pages. Extending it to other high-traffic pages (Emergency Replacements, KVA-spec product pages, the home-city Cincinnati location page, the competitor-replacement service pages) is a clear template-level opportunity covered under the Schema Implementation HIGH findings above.

Semantic HTML & Heading Structure

Semantic HTML5 tags (article, section, main, time, figure) give AI a structural map of your content beyond what schema describes. Heading hierarchy tells AI which ideas are primary and which are supporting. When the underlying HTML communicates “generic page” through heavy div/span usage, even rich JSON-LD has less to work with.

HIGH HTML Semantic Ratio 3% — Generic <div> and <span> Dominate the Markup

Wordlift’s independent AI-readiness audit measured the homepage’s semantic-HTML ratio at 3%. Entity Clarity

An entity is a real-world thing AI engines treat as a single identifiable unit — a company, a person, a product, a place — with its own properties and relationships to other entities. AI engines and search engines connect those entities into knowledge graphs to answer questions about you. When the connections are unambiguous and machine-readable, AI confidently describes and recommends the brand. When they’re fragmented or contradictory, the AI produces a vague answer or surfaces a competitor instead.
