Where this fits

An AI agent moves through your funnel just like a buyer does

The problem

AI agents have the same task needs as humans

The needs and goals of a visitor haven't changed. Whether they are human or an agent sent on behalf of a human, your visitor still needs your site to help them discover your products or services and determine whether they should buy from you instead of someone else.

But 42% of your buyers are beginning their search in an AI chatbox instead of a search engine. Humans are sending AI agents to your site to compare products and services, to pull specs, or to get quotes for them to evaluate before they visit a single site.

If an AI agent can't perform those actions on your site, they'll move on to one where they can. And you'll never even know they tried and failed.

Website stoppers

Your website is full of landmines for agents

Images, JavaScript, marketing jargon, product specs and attributes revealed only in a phone call with Sales: your whole internet sales process is a field of landmines to AI agents.

Everything I spell out in an Audit or Roadmap is designed to make their journey as fast and effortless as possible. Even if we've acted on every step, from helping them find your most important content to ensuring they can understand and trust what they find, you could still find yourself stopped just short of a sale.

Your quote form often sits behind a CAPTCHA built to keep bots out, and to that system an agent is a bot. Its rules often live in JavaScript, and the major AI crawlers do not run JavaScript at all. And nothing on the page tells the agent what a valid request even is.

A salesperson can rescue a vague inquiry with a phone call: "I'll need the phase and the enclosure rating." An agent cannot. A human tolerates ambiguity. An agent needs structure, up front, in a form it can read.

What an agent needs to ask you for

The contract spells out the information a buyer's agent has to supply — and it looks different depending on what the buyer is doing: getting a price, or sourcing a solution.

Example: a Request for QuoteRFQ · price a known spec

The buyer knows what they want and needs a price. To return one without a phone call, you need the agent to tell you, in a form you can read:

  • What the product is — the type and class (for a maker like Elsco, a dry-type transformer)
  • Its defining specifications — the handful of parameters that actually change the price: power rating, input and output voltage, how it is wired
  • Delivery requirements — quantity, and how fast they need it
  • Who is asking — company and contact, so you can respond

Your Action publishes this as a contract, so the agent sends one complete, valid request instead of abandoning a form it cannot read.

Example: a Request for ProposalRFP · source a solution

A proposal is bigger than a price. When the buyer is describing a need rather than a part number, the agent has to assemble:

  • The problem to solve — the outcome they want, not just a model number
  • Scope and requirements — what is in, what is out, constraints, standards to meet
  • Evaluation criteria — how they will choose: price, lead time, support, track record
  • Timeline and budget range
  • Who is asking — and how they want to be responded to

The same machine-readable approach tells an agent what a complete proposal request looks like for your business — so you make the shortlist before a human ever picks up the phone.

No rebuild. No new software. No backend changes. It works with any AI agent that respects open standards, not one vendor's platform. You own every file. Install once, keep forever.

To be truly ready for a web where AI agents outnumber human visitors, your business will need to operate on three layers:

  • Layer one is your visual website, for people and for search.
  • Layer two is HTML code and structured data; facts marked up so machines read them correctly.
  • Layer three is the new one: a machine-readable place where an agent can discover your full specification catalog, what it has to provide to request a quote, and how to submit that request.

This third layer is the plug your buyer's agent was looking for. Almost no one has built it yet, giving you a first-mover advantage.
