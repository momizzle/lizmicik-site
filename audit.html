You built your website for humans, but they aren't the only ones visiting it today. If what you've built can't be read or understood by AI agents, all your hard work goes down the drain.

Why This Matters Now

AI Agents Don't Read Like Humans Do

AI agents, language models, and answer engines are replacing the browser as the primary way businesses get discovered and evaluated. Your customers are asking ChatGPT or Claude before they Google.

Before any of those agents and engines will recommend your site — or do business through it — they must be able to:

Find it
Are you blocking them on purpose or by mistake?
Understand it
Is your content built in a way they can interpret?
Trust it
Can you be the authority they recommend?
Act on it
Can agents actually do business with you?

Only if your website passes all four of these readiness levels can it participate in an Internet economy projected to reach more than $1 trillion in commerce orchestrated by AI agents by 2030.

The Framework

Four questions. Every layer that matters.

The Agent Readiness Audit evaluates your site against a proprietary framework built from real-world testing across hundreds of AI agent interactions. It's not a checklist -it's a diagnostic that tells you what's broken, why it matters, and what to prioritize.

1

Can agents find you?

The infrastructure layer. Are AI crawlers welcomed or blocked? Can agents actually access and render your content? Do you have the signals that tell AI agents your site is open for business, or are you accidentally invisible?

"Every major infrastructure shift resets the findability problem. I'll check whether AI crawlers can actually reach and render your content, from robots.txt directives to JavaScript compilation to the new agent discovery protocols."

2

Can agents understand you?

The structure layer. Is your content semantically organized so agents can interpret it, not just read it? Schema markup, entity relationships, content hierarchy, and structured data turn a website from a wall of text into a knowledge source agents can work with. Most companies fail here.

"Most companies think having structured content means having formatted content. They're not the same thing. I evaluate whether your schema, semantic markup, and content hierarchy actually give AI agents something they can work with, or just look organized to a human eye."

3

Can agents trust you?

The authority layer. When an AI agent decides whether to cite you, recommend you, or surface you in an answer, what signals does it find? This is where most businesses discover their biggest blind spot.

"This is the blind spot. AI agents verify what your site claims by checking it against every other source they can find. I test what four major AI platforms actually say about your business today and trace where the inconsistencies are coming from."

4

Can agents do anything with you?

The capability layer. Beyond reading you, can agents actually act on your business — request a quote, place an order, negotiate a contract? Agent-actionable surfaces, /.well-known/ endpoints, MCP servers, A2A, and payments protocols are what move you from being described in AI answers to being part of agent workflows.

"The agent web isn't just discovery anymore — it's action. I evaluate whether your site exposes agent-actionable surfaces and discoverable protocols, so when an agent decides to do something with your business, it can."

Here's what actual findings look like — from a real audit, client identity redacted.

Medium
Selective AI Crawler Management

Modern AI platforms each send their own named crawler to read your website. Explicitly allowing these crawlers by name signals that your site welcomes AI indexing and can improve crawl priority.

What we found This site has explicit rules for Claude's bots but does not address GPTBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended, Bytespider, or other AI crawlers. While technically allowed under the wildcard rule, adding explicit Allow directives for each signals intent and can improve how thoroughly they index your content.
Technical detail

AI crawlers not specifically addressed: GPTBot, Google-Extended, Bytespider, CCBot, Anthropic-AI, PerplexityBot, Amazonbot. These all fall under User-Agent: * (permissive). The selective Claude-only approach may be from an early implementation before other AI crawlers became standard.

Recommendation: Add explicit Allow: / directives for GPTBot, Google-Extended, PerplexityBot, Bytespider, CCBot, and Amazonbot.
High
Services Pages Lack Service Schema

Schema.org Service markup tells AI agents "this page describes a professional service." Without it, AI models must guess whether a page is about services, a blog post, or something else entirely.

What we found Both the services overview and service detail pages use generic WebPage schema. Neither declares Service or ProfessionalService type. When a prospect asks AI "What services does this firm offer?", the AI cannot cite structured schema and must parse unstructured text instead.
Technical detail

WordPress theme defaults to WebPage type for all pages. Yoast SEO does not auto-generate Service schema. Requires custom post type schema template in child theme.

Pages affected:

  • https://████████.com/service-areas — has_service: false
  • https://████████.com/service-areas/strategy-scale — has_service: false
Recommendation: Implement schema.org/Service on all service pages with properties: name, description, provider (Organization), areaServed, serviceType.
High
Zero Visibility Across All AI Models

AI visibility measures whether AI agents mention your firm when users ask questions in your industry. We test this by running structured conversations across multiple AI models and tracking which firms are mentioned.

What we found This firm has 0% visibility across all four AI model families tested: DeepSeek, Perplexity, Gemini, and OpenAI. In 80 AI conversations, the firm was never mentioned. This is not a ranking problem. The firm is completely absent from AI answers about their industry.
Technical detail

20 prompts generating 80 conversations across 4 model families. This site: 0 mentions across 397 total source citations. Top cited in category: bcg.com (23), mckinsey.com (22), youtube.com (17), pwc.com (17), deloitte.com (15), bain.com (15).

Recommendation: This finding is the result of compounding failures across Find and Understand dimensions. Fixing schema, content depth, and authority signals will begin building the foundation for visibility. Re-run testing in 90 days after implementing initial fixes.
High
No Agent-Actionable Surfaces Published

Agent-actionable surfaces at /.well-known/ tell AI agents what your site can do, not just what it says. Without them, an agent can describe your business in an answer but cannot transact with it, even when the buyer is ready to act.

What we found None of the expected agent-discovery files are published: /.well-known/api-catalog, /.well-known/mcp/server-card.json, /.well-known/agent-skills/index.json. The site has API documentation pages indexed for humans, but agents querying the standardized discovery paths get a 404. When an agent decides to take action on behalf of a buyer, it routes to a competitor that publishes the right contracts.
Technical detail

Three agent-discovery surfaces evaluated: API Catalog (RFC 9727) at /.well-known/api-catalog, MCP Server Card at /.well-known/mcp/server-card.json, and Agent Skills Index at /.well-known/agent-skills/index.json. None present on this site. OAuth discovery (RFC 8414 / RFC 9728) also absent, which rules out authenticated agent flows.

Recommendation: Publish at minimum /.well-known/api-catalog pointing to your public API spec. Add an MCP server card when ready to expose agent-callable tools. Add OAuth discovery when authenticated agent flows are scoped.
