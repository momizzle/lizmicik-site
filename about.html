Open three doors to new business

When I say I help complex B2B companies prepare their website, expertise, and conversion processes for AI agents, I mean three specific pieces of work.

Your website is what an agent reads first. Structure, semantic HTML, machine-readable facts, structured data. If an agent can't find your pages or understand what you do, nothing downstream matters.

Your expertise is what an agent decides to trust. The certifications, credentials, people, and proof that win you deals in person are usually locked in someone's head or in PDFs where no machine can read them. We just scanned eleven industrial and consulting sites: not one published a single machine-readable credential. The proof exists — it just isn't written down where the new buyer looks.

Your conversion processes are what an agent tries to use. The RFQ form, the quote request, the RFP intake flow — the moment a buyer's assistant stops researching and tries to act. Most were built assuming a human is filling in the boxes, and they fail silently when one isn't.

It can look like writing everything down twice — once for people, once for machines. It isn't. It's the same work, done once and done right, that gets you found, understood, trusted, and used by every visitor you have — human or agent. That's what drives your business.

The agentic web is that same shape of problem, one level up. This time the structure that has to change isn't just your website. It's how your expertise and your sales process show up to a machine.

What's at stake

Now the entire web is facing the biggest structural shift I've seen in my career. AI agents have become a primary surface for discovery. They read websites differently than people do, and they'll act on websites differently too.

The companies that figure out how to communicate clearly, completely, and consistently to AI agents will own the next decade of discovery. The ones that don't will watch their visibility erode and never quite know why.

I'm here to help clients lay the foundations they'll need to survive and thrive in this new agentic web. No one can know yet what it will fully become. But I couldn't be more excited to be part of the transition.

The Find / Understand / Trust / Use framework

I use a four-layer framework to diagnose websites for the agentic web. Each layer asks a different question, and each one can stop an AI agent cold on its own.

Find — Can AI access your site?

The infrastructure layer: robots.txt directives, CDN bot rules, JavaScript rendering, structured data access. If AI can't read your site, nothing else matters.

Read the FIND layer →

Understand — Can AI parse what you do?

The content layer: entity clarity, semantic HTML, schema markup, fact-over-vibe content. If AI can read you but can't interpret you, it fills the gaps with assumptions.

Read the UNDERSTAND layer →

Trust — Will AI cite you over your competitors?

The authority layer: consistent entity signals across the open web, corroborating sources, fresh and definitive content. If AI doesn't trust you, it cites someone else.

Read the TRUST layer →

Use — Can AI act on your business, or just describe it?

The capability layer: agent-actionable surfaces, /.well-known/ endpoints, MCP servers, A2A and payments protocols. If AI can find, understand, and trust you but can't do anything with you, the deal goes to the company whose website knows how to negotiate.
