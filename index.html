The Shift

You built a website to support your business. Over the years you worked hard to optimize it for the search engines so even more people could find it. You learned to use social media and video to expand your reach even further. Then you went to work testing it — crafting each word and each image to build a loyal customer base.

Now the web is being re-read — not by people, but by AI agents. The rules of success are being rewritten by large language models to serve answer engines that are quietly replacing the browser as the primary way businesses get found and evaluated.

51% of web traffic is now bots.

For the first time, automated traffic has surpassed human visitors. The web your website was built for is no longer the only web that matters.

Source: Imperva 2025 Bad Bot Report

You may worry that your website will become invisible to these systems. Many websites will, if they aren't re-optimized to communicate structured, verifiable information to AI. The result: agents either won't be able to find them, won't understand what they do, or won't trust what they find enough to recommend them.

The companies that figure out how to talk to agents — clearly, consistently, and credibly — will own the next decade of discovery.

The ones that don't will watch their visibility erode and never understand why.

The Framework

I evaluate readiness for the agentic web across three layers:

Can agents find you?

The infrastructure layer. Are AI crawlers blocked or welcomed? Can agents actually access and render your content? Do you have the signals — robots.txt directives, llms.txt, proper rendering — that tell agents your site is open for business?

Can agents understand you?

The structure layer. Is your content semantically organized so agents can parse it — not just read it, but interpret it? Schema markup, entity relationships, content architecture, and structured data turn a website from a wall of text into a knowledge source agents can work with.

Can agents trust you?

The authority layer. When an AI agent decides whether to cite you, recommend you, or surface you in an answer — what does it find? Earned media, consistent entity signals across platforms, and verified claims are what separate the businesses agents trust from the ones they ignore.
